Tropic Tumble is a drag-to-match beach game I designed and built end to end: the rules, the levels, the economy, the interface, the sound and the code. Pick a device, play a level, then picture your brand in it.

Made for devices

One build, every screen

Switch devices above and the game re-lays itself out live: the pile gets taller or wider, stickers resize, and the art stays anchored. Nothing is a screenshot. It is the same build your players would get.

Fits what it is given

Small phones, tall phones and tablets share one layout that measures its space and adapts, including while you are playing.

Touch first

Drag, drop and tap are built on pointer input, so it plays the same with a thumb, a stylus or a mouse, with haptic taps on phones that support them.

Light and instant

About 6.5 MB including eight painted worlds and a campaign map, with no install and no engine download. There are no audio files at all: the music and every sound effect are generated in the browser.

What is in the build

Small game, finished systems

A mini game only earns its place in an app when the loop is complete. Everything below is wired and working in the build above.

17sticker designs across 4 packs
40levels on a campaign map across eight islands
0engines, libraries or audio files: one hand-written file
5device sizes on this page
  • A physics pile. Stickers fall, stack and settle with a custom solver tuned to feel soft and readable.
  • Drag to match. Find the twin, drop one on the other, and both fly to their pack. A wrong drop gets a wobble, not a penalty.
  • Pressure and blockers. From level 2 a tide keeps dropping new stickers, and the level is lost if the pile rests above the line. Ice locks stickers until you match beside them, and thick ice takes two.
  • Levels that ramp. Each level adds designs and quickens the tide, and from level 6 the goal rotates: fill the packs, melt the ice, reach a score or outlast a storm. Scores earn up to three stars, and any level can be replayed.
  • Bonuses and boosters. Combo, Super Combo, Streak, Chain Bonus and Match Rush reward quick, clean play. Hint, Shuffle and Magnet help when you are stuck.
  • Reasons to come back. A sticker album with bronze, silver and gold tiers, a chest for every ten stars, a daily reward that grows over a seven-day streak, and saving between visits.
  • A working economy. Energy and gems each have clear ways to be earned and spent. The shop sells gem packs, a starter pack and ad removal.
  • Ads where players choose them. Watch one for energy, a second chance or double gems. A short ad between levels disappears for anyone who buys ad removal.
  • Ready for real payments. Purchases and ads run in a clearly labelled test mode here. Each sits behind a single function, which is where the store and ad network plug in.
  • Music and sound. An island loop over ocean waves, and a match sound that climbs a note with every step of a combo.
  • Game feel. Sparkle bursts, confetti, pop-in rewards and a fox that reacts when you tap it.
  • Eight worlds and a campaign map. A new painted scene every five levels, laid out as a map you can pan around, each with its own life: waterfalls and mist, embers off the lava, fireflies at night, bubbles on the reef.
  • A full interface. HUD, pause, tutorial tips and a how-to-play screen, shop, album, campaign map, a banner for every message, and loading and level-complete screens.
  • Balance checked by bots. Simulated players run each level at three skill levels, so the difficulty curve is tuned on numbers before people play it.
  • Testing tools. The gear menu can add energy, finish a level or reset the save. Try it.

Working with your team

How I would build yours

This is the process Tropic Tumble followed. It is designed so your producers, artists and engineers can see and steer the game from the first day.

Start from your reference

A game you admire, a brief, or just a brand. I break the loop down into rules we can agree on before anything is drawn.

Playable on a phone first

You get a link the whole team can open on their own phones. Tropic Tumble had a first playable the same day it started, and every decision after that was made by playing it.

Your art, dropped in

Backgrounds, panels, icons and stickers here were swapped in one piece at a time without touching the game logic. Your artists keep working in their own tools.

Feel, then polish

Micro animations, effects, sound and reward moments go in once the loop is fun, and each one is tested on a phone-sized screen before it stays.

Hand-off that fits your pipeline

A readable, commented codebase with the level curve, prices and rewards in one place, and payments and ads each behind one function. It can ship on the web as is, be wrapped for the app stores, or serve as the spec for a Unity, Godot or Unreal build. An Android project is already set up around this one.
